## Goals

- Reach every streaming service they use from one place, in the interface they know from the TV.
- Watch full screen, and go back and forth between the launcher and the services without Chrome's
  tabs or address bar coming into view.
- Find which service has a show or film without searching each one.
- Keep Chrome a browser: the launcher comes up when asked for, and never takes over browsing.

## Skills and tools

- A software developer: comfortable loading an unpacked extension in Chrome's developer mode, and
  with keyboard shortcuts.
- A Chromebook with ChromeOS and Chrome 150, its keyboard and touchpad.
- Streaming services used on it include Prime Video, YouTube, MUBI, Tubi, and Plex; no Netflix
  subscription.

## Context of use

Alone, on their own Chromebook, between or alongside ordinary browsing. They switch to watching on
purpose, want the screen to themselves while they do, and go back to browsing afterwards. Nobody
else uses the Chromebook, so nothing needs guarding against another person's accidental changes.

## Frustrations

- Each service is a separate website, with no one place that gathers them as the TV's quiet home
  screen does.
- In full screen, switching tabs briefly shows Chrome's tabs and address bar.
- A video goes on playing behind whatever they switch to.
- A Google search for a title does not reliably say which service has it.
